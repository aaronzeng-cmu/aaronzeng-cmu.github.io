Three directions for your website

Each sample is a full homepage built from your real content: bio, news, publications, and photos. Hover a preview and click to open it, then use the theme button in each sample to check light and dark. Nothing here touches the published site.

What's working

  • Accurate, focused content. The bio, news, and publications all tell one coherent research story.
  • Rich publication data: abstract, BibTeX, DOI, and the MIT News link are already in the .bib file.
  • Clean, legible baseline with working dark mode, search, and a structured CV.

What's holding it back

  • It looks like stock al-folio: same Roboto, purple/cyan accent, and layout as thousands of academic sites. Nothing visual connects to sleep, EEG, or CSF.
  • The bio is one ~170-word paragraph mixing research, the paper, and three degrees. It's hard to get the point in 10 seconds.
  • Your best material is buried. The Sci. Transl. Med. paper and its MIT News coverage are an inline link. The SfN talk photo appears only on the publications page, and the fellowships only in the CV.
  • The photo sits above a monospace address block that reads like a code snippet. Links would be more useful there than a mailing address.
  • The fixed footer bar always covers the bottom of the screen, and the large social icons at the end feel disconnected.
  • The homepage is thin below the fold: two news items and no selected publications (selected_papers: false). Solid-color venue badges and large colored section headers compete with the paper titles.

The directions

Direction 1

Scholar

Quiet, editorial, timeless. Serif type on warm paper, one deep-red accent, and generous whitespace. It's the same site, edited and typeset properly.

Type
Newsreader (serif) + Inter for labels
Palette
Paper white, ink, oxblood accent
Effort
Low. Mostly theme and CSS.
  • A one-sentence lede and a two-paragraph bio. Degrees move into an Education list.
  • Labeled sections in a left margin: Research, Recent, Selected work
  • Selected publications on the homepage with a featured paper and a press callout
  • Outlined venue tags and text links instead of solid badges and buttons
  • The footer scrolls with the page instead of being fixed

Trade-off: it's the safest option and the most "professor-ready," but also the least memorable. It stays inside al-folio's structure, so updates and features keep working.

Direction 2

Slow Wave

Your research becomes the visual identity. A night-toned, instrument-like site with a live EEG slow-wave trace and stimulus markers at each peak, the idea behind your closed-loop work.

Type
IBM Plex Sans + Plex Mono
Palette
Night navy, signal teal, stimulus amber (dark-first, light available)
Effort
Medium. New hero and a custom about layout.
  • An animated trace hero with a legend that explains it (it respects reduced-motion settings)
  • A 4-step "how it works" pipeline: EEG → phase prediction → sound → CSF
  • A featured-paper card with citation, DOI, and press
  • The news becomes a dated log, and there's a "Path" timeline: UCSD → CMU → Pitt → MIT

Trade-off: the most distinctive option, and it explains your work at a glance. It's tied to your current research, so the motif may need refreshing if your focus shifts.

Direction 3

Bento

A modern, photo-forward portfolio. The homepage is a grid of rounded tiles: intro, portrait, featured paper, talk photo, education, news, and your HTMAA work.

Type
Bricolage Grotesque + Inter
Palette
Warm grey, white cards, indigo accent
Effort
Higher. New homepage layout.
  • A first-person headline and quick links (CV, GitHub, LinkedIn, ORCID) above the fold
  • The SfN talk photo and the paper become hero tiles, not footnotes
  • The HTMAA "currently making" tile adds a personal, hands-on side
  • Card-style publications and a bold "Say hello" contact block

Trade-off: the most approachable option, and it reads well to industry and the public. It needs a steady supply of photos and news to keep tiles from looking empty, and it's furthest from al-folio's defaults.

Mix and match

The pieces are portable. For example, you could use Scholar's typography with Slow Wave's trace as a thin banner, or Bento's tiles for news and the talk photo under a Scholar header.

Some improvements apply whichever direction you pick: split the bio, show selected publications on the homepage (selected_papers: true), surface the MIT News coverage, unfix the footer (footer_fixed: false), and add your fellowships to the homepage.

The headlines and taglines in the samples are draft copy adapted from your bio. Edit them freely.

These sample files live in _design-samples/. Jekyll skips folders that start with an underscore, so they won't be published.
